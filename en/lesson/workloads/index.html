Workloads: DaemonSet, StatefulSet, Job and more

Not every app is a Deployment. Agents, databases, tasks that finish, and release strategies.

Step by step

Step 1 · ContextDeployment: for stateless apps

A Deployment is perfect for stateless apps, like the store’s API: the replicas are identical and any of them can be swapped for another.

But some cases don’t fit: an agent that must run on every machine, a database where each replica is different, a task that should finish.

Step 2 · ConceptDaemonSet: one pod on every node

A DaemonSet guarantees exactly one pod on every node (or on every node matching a selector). When a new node joins, it gets its pod right away.

It’s the right tool for log agents (Fluent Bit), monitoring (node-exporter) and networking (the CNI itself and kube-proxy).

Good to know: DaemonSets ignore a replica count: the number of nodes decides how many pods exist.

kubectl get ds -A

Step 3 · ConceptStatefulSet: every replica is unique

Databases need each replica to have a fixed identity. A StatefulSet creates pods with stable names (db-0, db-1, db-2) and, with volumeClaimTemplates, a PVC of its own for each.

If db-1 dies, a new db-1 is born and mounts the same data-db-1 disk. A headless Service gives each replica the address db-1.db.

Key idea: Deployment: interchangeable replicas. StatefulSet: replicas with their own name, order and disk.

Step 4 · ConceptA StatefulSet starts in order

By default, the StatefulSet starts db-0, waits until it’s ready, then db-1, and only then db-2. To shut down or scale in, it goes in reverse.

That lets the database elect its primary before the replicas join.

Step 5 · ConceptJob: a task that finishes

A Job runs pods until the task is done, then stops. completions says how many times it must succeed; parallelism, how many run at once.

Job pods use restartPolicy Never or OnFailure. When they finish they stay Completed so you can read the logs.

kubectl create job relatorio --image=busybox -- sh -c "echo ok"

Step 6 · ProblemWhen the Job fails

If the pods fail, the Job retries, waiting longer each time. After backoffLimit attempts (6 by default), it gives up and marks the Job Failed.

activeDeadlineSeconds caps the total time, and ttlSecondsAfterFinished deletes the Job on its own after a while.

Watch out: Without a backoffLimit or deadline, a buggy Job can keep creating pods for a long time.

Step 7 · ConceptCronJob: a task on a schedule

A CronJob creates a new Job at every scheduled time. The format is Linux cron: "0 3 * * *" is every day at 3 a.m.

concurrencyPolicy says what to do if the previous run hasn’t finished: allow it (Allow), skip (Forbid) or replace (Replace).

ExpressionWhen
*/5 * * * *Every 5 minutes
0 3 * * *Every day at 3 a.m.
0 0 * * 0Sunday at midnight
kubectl create cronjob backup --image=busybox --schedule="0 3 * * *" -- sh -c "date"

Step 8 · ConceptInit containers: getting ready first

Init containers run before the main container, one at a time, and must finish successfully. Only then does the app start.

Typical uses: waiting for the database to answer, fetching a config, fixing a volume’s permissions.

Watch out: Pod stuck in Init:0/1? The init container hasn’t finished. Read its log with kubectl logs pod -c init-name.

Step 9 · ConceptSidecars and other patterns

A sidecar runs next to the app in the same pod: a proxy, a log collector. In the native form, it’s declared in initContainers with restartPolicy: Always: it starts before the app and keeps running alongside it.

Other patterns: adapter (translates the app’s output into another format) and ambassador (a local proxy to outside services).

Step 10 · ConceptBlue/green: switch everything at once

You run two complete Deployments: blue (v1, in production) and green (v2, new). The Service points to one of them through the version label.

When green is tested, change the Service selector to version=v2. The switch is instant, and going back is just changing the selector again.

Good to know: It costs double the pods during the switch, but rollback takes a second.

kubectl patch svc loja -p '{"spec":{"selector":{"version":"v2"}}}'

Step 11 · ConceptCanary: testing with a few customers

Two Deployments with the same app=loja label: three v1 pods and one v2 pod. The Service sends traffic to all four, so roughly 25% of customers use the new version.

If all goes well, raise v2’s replicas and lower v1’s until the switch is complete.

Good to know: For exact percentages without juggling replicas, the Gateway API supports weights on routes.

Step 12 · SummarySummary: which workload to use

Choose by the nature of the work, not by habit.
